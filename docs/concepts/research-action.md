# From a finding to action

<div class="in-brief" markdown>
**In brief.** A finding changes nothing by itself. It leaves Research when you take it somewhere: into the **Journal**, as a dated record; to the person who can act on it, as the notebook **exported as a page**; or to another owner, as a notebook they run on their flights. What you then *do* — fly differently, call someone, change a part, nothing — is a decision between you, the Operators Manual and the people who built the parts.
</div>

## Three ways out

**The Journal.** Ask Claude to note the finding in the Journal when it finishes — the one entry Claude may write there. It becomes a *study finding* entry with the date and a link to the notebook. The Journal is the record of what you decided and why; a finding that led to a call, a change or a deliberate decision to do nothing belongs there. Then mark your question *Watch* or *Acted*, with a note, in the inbox.

**A page for the person who can act.** *Export as a page (.html)* on the notebook gives one file anyone can open: the question, the narrative, every number beside its reference, the tables and figures. In the oscillation case the propeller controller's manufacturer recognised a known behaviour from one email with a plot and the per-takeoff table, and offered a firmware update the same day.

**Another owner.** Export the notebook (.zip); another owner of the same engine imports it and runs it on their flights ([Notebooks you keep and share](research-studies.md)). Two aircraft showing the same shape is a different conversation with a manufacturer from one.

## Writing to a manufacturer

The notebook answers the questions a support engineer asks first. Add what it cannot know:

- the installation — engine, propeller and controller models and serials, firmware versions, the avionics that wrote the logs;
- what you felt in the aircraft, when, and in what conditions;
- the one question you want answered.

Keep the finding's count as EIS wrote it (*in 93 of 98 takeoffs*); it is the most persuasive thing in the message.

## What a finding is not

It is not an airworthiness judgement, and Research does not make one. Whether a repeating rpm shape is acceptable for flight is answered by the Operators Manual's limits (which the app checks separately, under Limits and events), by the manufacturers of the engine and the governing parts, and by you.

Spec 06 §10.3 and Spec 16 §5 define the ways out.
