    SlingologyEIS¶

    Engine health analytics for Rotax iS aircraft engines — built for how a FADEC engine actually works.

    SlingologyEIS reads the engine logs your Garmin avionics already record on every flight and answers two questions about each one:

    1. Did anything go outside the Rotax Operators Manual limits?
    2. Was this flight unusual for your engine?

    The limits come from Rotax. The idea of "usual" comes from your own flight history. What to do about either is your call — the app never silences anything on its own, and it never stops watching something you've told it to tolerate.

    Open SlingologyEIS — it runs entirely in your browser. There is nothing to install and no account; the analysis happens on your computer and the results stay there. Your logs stay in your own folders — the app reads them; it never copies or uploads them. If you would rather run it as a program on your own machine, you can (how).

    Keep a copy of your workspace

    Your workspaces are kept by your browser, on this device. Clearing the site's data removes them, and a browser short of space may clear them on its own. Settings → App → Export workspace… writes one file that restores everything — make one after your first analysis and whenever you have made decisions you would not want to redo. How.

    No logs yet? Try the sample workspace

    Download the sample workspace (10 MB) — a season of N117ZS, the Sling TSi the app was developed on: 19 flights, the three filters its owner set and why, and one annotation. Open the app, go to Settings → App → Import workspace… and pick the zip; the app analyses the flights in about a minute. Then look at Trends for the widening EGT spread, open the 2 September flight to see the fuel-pressure filter at work, and read the Journal. The sample is the same workspace the screenshots on this site were taken from.

    What this app is, and isn't¶

    SlingologyEIS is a reading aid for the data your engine already produces. It will show you when a reading went past a limit, when a flight looks different from your own history, and when something about your engine has been slowly changing. It will not tell you whether your aircraft is airworthy, and it does not replace the Operators Manual, your maintenance schedule or your mechanic. When the app says something is worth raising with your mechanic, that is what it means — a conversation starter with the evidence attached, not a diagnosis.

    Which engines¶

    Rotax 912iS, 915iS and 916iS. The Operators Manual limits for all three are built in and verified against the manuals. The analytics themselves were developed and validated on a 916iS; on the other engines each topic is marked unverified until there is data from such an aircraft. See The topics for the status of each.

    Which logs¶

    Garmin G3X logs copied from the SD card, Garmin Pilot exports, and Garmin AXIS exports. The app recognises the format from the file itself, and a workspace can mix them.

    Free and open source under the MIT licence. The project, its source code and the research paper behind the analytics are at github.com/AlchemyGeek/Rotax9iSEIS.
