    Investigation prompts¶

    The prompts the EIS extension offers in Claude Desktop (Spec 16 §4.6): pick one from the + menu in a chat, fill in what it asks, and Claude starts with the EIS method in front of it. Each is shown below as Claude receives it, with the placeholders in braces.

    Investigate¶

    Investigate a question about the engine in a notebook, following the EIS method.

    Asks for
    question The question, in your words (e.g. "Is there an oscillation during takeoff and initial climb?"), or the title of one you marked on the Flight view; leave it empty and Claude takes your newest open question
    The prompt

    Investigate this question about my engine with SlingologyEIS:

    {{question}}

    Which question: call guide first — it lists the questions I marked on the Flight view (Mark for Claude). If the question above is empty, take my newest open question (pins_list names it newest_open); if I have none, ask me what to investigate — you may suggest what my open inbox candidates (inbox_list) and recent flights point to — and wait for my answer. If the text above names one of my questions (its title, or near it), take that one. Otherwise it is a new question in my words.

    1. Call guide first and follow its method and writing rules.
    2. Find the flight or flights the question names with flights_find (or flights_list), and read aircraft_get — my notes say what changed and when.
    3. Start a notebook with study_create: for one of my questions pass its question_id, and the notebook opens with my marks — my note, then a log view of the window — so start from what I saw; otherwise the question as I asked it. Write the plan under How this was done ("section": 5) before you measure.
    4. Look at one flight first (a log view of the window, eis.load), then across all my flights with a slice rule, measuring what the question is about — the guide's table says which instrument fits (a test only when the question is about steadiness, coupling, a difference or an outlier; otherwise load, slice, plot and look). Each finding is one sentence and one evidence cell (eis.table, eis.figure, a log view, each with a caption); a test used for another reason says why beside it. Explore in scratch cells.
    5. Read what my manuals say about it (documents_list, document_read) and cite the pages. Anything from outside my logs and documents (forums, other owners) is context, under How this was done, labelled so.
    6. State the mechanism as a hypothesis and name the alternatives you ruled out, each with its evidence, under How this was done.
    7. Finish with the structured Answer: study_finish with question_scope (flights and dates, references, what was taken as given, what the logs can't tell), shows (3–5 sentences with numbers — the first is my Journal's line), means (the hypothesis, your confidence, what would change it) and actions (what · who · what it would settle; recheck_after_flights when EIS can watch it; or actions_none and why); question when it was one of mine, so it and its marks are resolved; tags: two or three topic words for what the notebook is about (not what it found).

    The notebook is the record (the guide's The notebook is the record): every finding is one sentence and one evidence cell (eis.table, eis.figure or a log view, with a caption — never a print); the plan and the method go under How this was done; exploring is scratch. A quick look to decide where to go next is a probe and stays in the chat. If we talk on after you finish and it changes what the notebook says — or I say "put that in the notebook" — open a follow-up (study_followup), add its cells, and study_finish again with the Answer rewritten as it now stands and changed saying what changed above.

    Finish with the structured Answer.

    Look at this inbox item¶

    Investigate one candidate the EIS detectors raised and resolve it.

    Asks for
    candidate_id required — The inbox candidate's id (inbox_list shows them)
    The prompt

    Look at inbox candidate {{candidate_id}} for me.

    1. Call guide first and follow its method.
    2. Read the candidate with inbox_list: its preset, measurement, the slices involved and the evidence.
    3. Start a notebook with study_create, passing inbox_candidate: "{{candidate_id}}", and write the plan under How this was done.
    4. Look at one of its slices in one flight (a log view), then measure across the flights what the candidate raised (the same preset, the same measurement — the guide's table names its instrument) and check the alternatives that fit it: one log source only, one flight only, the 1 Hz sampling floor for a period, altitude or temperature.
    5. Finish with the structured Answer — study_finish with question_scope, shows, means, actions (or actions_none) — resolving the candidate (resolution: nothing-here, watch or acted) with a one-line note saying why.

    The notebook is the record (the guide's The notebook is the record): every finding is one sentence and one evidence cell (eis.table, eis.figure or a log view, with a caption — never a print); the plan and the method go under How this was done; exploring is scratch. A quick look to decide where to go next is a probe and stays in the chat. If we talk on after you finish and it changes what the notebook says — or I say "put that in the notebook" — open a follow-up (study_followup), add its cells, and study_finish again with the Answer rewritten as it now stands and changed saying what changed above.

    Before / after¶

    Compare the engine before and after a change (a date, or the change in words).

    Asks for
    change required — The date of the change (YYYY-MM-DD) or the change in words (e.g. "the new propeller")
    The prompt

    Compare my engine before and after this change: {{change}}.

    1. Call guide first and follow its method.
    2. If I gave the change in words, find its date in my notes (aircraft_get) or the Journal (journal_list); if you can't, ask me.
    3. Start a notebook with study_create, and write the plan under How this was done before measuring.
    4. For the conditions the change could affect (full power, governed climb, stable cruise), compare the slices before and after with groups split by date — eis.groups("full_power", "rpm.swing", by="date", date="…") — for the measurements the change could have moved (for a propeller: rpm, its swing and period, MAP, fuel flow; for an oil change: oil temperature and pressure). Say why each measurement was chosen.
    5. Each thing that changed or did not is a finding: one sentence with its numbers and the group sizes, and its evidence table. The caveats (fewer flights after, different seasons or sources) go in question_scope. Finish with the structured Answer — study_finish with question_scope, shows, means and actions (or actions_none).

    The notebook is the record (the guide's The notebook is the record): every finding is one sentence and one evidence cell (eis.table, eis.figure or a log view, with a caption — never a print); the plan and the method go under How this was done; exploring is scratch. A quick look to decide where to go next is a probe and stays in the chat. If we talk on after you finish and it changes what the notebook says — or I say "put that in the notebook" — open a follow-up (study_followup), add its cells, and study_finish again with the Answer rewritten as it now stands and changed saying what changed above.

    Review new flights¶

    A short review of the flights since the last study finding, the inbox, and anything that stands out.

    The prompt

    Review my new flights.

    1. Call guide first.
    2. Find the flights since my last study finding (journal_list shows the study findings; flights_list with date_from), and read the inbox (inbox_list) and the questions I marked (pins_list).
    3. Start a short notebook with study_create ("Review of flights since …").
    4. For each new flight: one line on where it went and anything that stands out (events, Trends shifts, and eis.unusual — this review is about what stands out). For each open inbox candidate and each open question of mine: what it is, and whether it needs a closer look (Investigate takes a question).
    5. Finish with the structured Answer, briefly: shows — what stands out; means — what needs a closer look and what doesn't; actions — each closer look as what · who · what it would settle, or actions_none.

    The notebook is the record (the guide's The notebook is the record): every finding is one sentence and one evidence cell (eis.table, eis.figure or a log view, with a caption — never a print); the plan and the method go under How this was done; exploring is scratch. A quick look to decide where to go next is a probe and stays in the chat. If we talk on after you finish and it changes what the notebook says — or I say "put that in the notebook" — open a follow-up (study_followup), add its cells, and study_finish again with the Answer rewritten as it now stands and changed saying what changed above.
